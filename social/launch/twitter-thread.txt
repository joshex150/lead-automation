1/
For the first stretch of running our studio, my entire client pipeline was other people.

Someone would refer us. A business would find us. Work came in and I never had to go looking for it.

That's lovely, right up until the month it goes quiet.

[attach image: 01-title.png]

---

2/
Referrals are somebody else's decision. You can't schedule them.

At some point waiting stopped being a plan. If clients weren't going to come to me, I had to go to them.

Which is when I found out how much work that actually is.

---

3/
Open Google Maps. Search "restaurants in Lagos". Click a listing. Click through to the site. Watch it load perfectly well. Go back. Next one.

Most of what I opened was fine. The ones that genuinely needed a website were buried somewhere in all that clicking.

---

4/
Then came the writing.

The first few messages were good. After that I'd start copying the last one and changing the name.

Which is exactly the sort of email I delete when it lands in my own inbox.

---

5/
So I built the thing I kept wishing I had. YEAN Leads.

It pulls businesses from Google Places by city and category, then opens what each one actually has online.

DNS. Certificate. HTTP status. Response time. Mobile viewport. Broken internal links.

[attach image: 03-score.png]

---

6/
If the listing points at a Linktree, it follows the link and checks whether a real site is hiding behind it.

Telling somebody they have no website when they do is the fastest way to lose the reply.

---

7/
Then it scores what it found.

No website: +40
Site is down: +40
Social media only: +30
Opened recently: +25

I set the weights for us. You'd set your own. Anything over the threshold lands in a queue.

---

8/
The message is drafted from that business's actual situation.

An email reads like an email.
A WhatsApp message is shorter and doesn't sign off like a letter.

Then it stops and waits for me.

[attach image: 04-message.png]

---

9/
That last part matters more to me than the rest.

Nothing goes out without a person reading it and pressing approve. One follow-up, ever, and only if there was no reply.

I wasn't building a spam cannon. I was trying to stop waiting for the phone to ring.

10/
